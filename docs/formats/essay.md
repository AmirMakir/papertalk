# Format: essay

Blog posts, opinion pieces, company announcements and policy posts: Anthropic news and research posts, personal essays such as "Machines of Loving Grace", newsletters. 1.5–2 minutes, about 16–20 lines.

An essay argues. The viewer leaves knowing what the author claims, why they believe it, and what it would change, without the video taking a side.

| Beat | Lines | What the picture does |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook: the claim** | 2 | The essay's boldest sentence, close to the author's words, with who says it. If the author has a concrete image or example, start from it. |
| **The picture** | 1–2 | Draw the claim as one picture the argument can build on: a timeline, a before/after, a scale, a map of who is affected. |
| **The reasons** | 4–6 | Two or three reasons the author gives, each added to the picture as it is said. Quote short phrases on screen, attributed. |
| **The pushback** | 2 | The strongest objection the essay itself takes on, or the open question it leaves. |
| **What it changes** | 2 | What the author says follows: for people, for builders, for policy. |
| **Map** | 3 | Where each part sits in the essay (section headings). |
| **End** | 1 | Title, author, link. |

Do:
- Attribute every opinion: "Dario argues", "the post says", "Anthropic's position is".
- Keep facts and opinions apart. A number from the essay is still the essay's number: say whose it is.
- Quote briefly: a phrase or one sentence, never paragraphs.

Don't:
- Present the video as coming from the author or their company.
- Turn the essay into a list of its sections. The map at the end is the only list.

Kit needs for this format (see ROADMAP): a quote card, an argument picture that builds (claim with reasons hanging off it), a timeline.
