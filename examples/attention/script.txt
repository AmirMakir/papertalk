# A Hook
"The dog ignored the ball because it was tired."
You knew at once that "it" is the dog. You just linked two words, five words apart.
# B Old way
In {2017|twenty seventeen}, the standard translation models couldn't do that directly. They read one word at a time and passed a memory along a chain.
So the further apart two words were, the more steps their link had to survive.
And each step had to wait for the one before, so a sentence couldn't be processed in parallel.
# C The bet
There was already a fix, called attention: a shortcut that let a model look straight at any word, however far. But it was bolted onto the chain.
So this paper did not invent attention. Its claim is the title: attention is all you need. Remove the chain. Keep only the shortcut.
# D Mechanism
Here's how it works. Take the word "it". To find what it refers to, the word sends out a query: a question.
Every word answers with a key: a label. Compare the question with each label and you get a score: how relevant is this word?
But raw scores can get huge, and with huge scores the model almost stops learning. So the paper divides them by a fixed number,
then turns them into shares that add up to {100%|one hundred percent}. That step is called softmax.
Each word also holds a value: what it actually carries. "It" takes a blend of the values, weighted by those shares: mostly "dog", a little of the rest.
That's attention. On paper, it's one line.
Every word does this at once. No waiting. And since the sentence is attending to itself, the paper calls it self-attention.
# E Two fixes
But a word relates to the others in more than one way. So the model runs eight of these side by side. The paper calls them heads.
And there's a catch: with no chain, word order is gone. "Dog ignored ball" and "ball ignored dog" look the same. So each word gets a position signal added.
Stack six layers of this to read the sentence: the encoder. Six more to write the translation: the decoder. The paper calls the whole model the Transformer.
# F Payoff
What did removing the chain buy? Any two words are now one step apart. And the work runs in parallel.
The smaller model trained in {12|twelve} hours on eight GPUs. The larger one took three and a half days and beat the best English-to-German translation results of its time.
The price: every word looks at every word, so the cost grows with the square of the sentence length.
